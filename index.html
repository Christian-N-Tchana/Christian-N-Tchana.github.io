About me

I am an ecologist and PhD researcher at La Rochelle University (CEBC–CNRS, France), with a particular interest in avian ecology, host–parasite interactions, and biodiversity conservation.

My research explores how environmental changes and human activities influence bird communities, their parasites, and associated ecological processes.

I am particularly interested in the effects of agricultural intensification, pesticide exposure, and habitat modification on wildlife health and biodiversity. My current PhD focuses on the relationships between farming practices, parasitism, and zoonotic pathogens in farmland birds.

Beyond my doctoral research, I am involved in ornithological research and conservation initiatives in Cameroon, particularly in agricultural and urban landscapes.

Current projects

Agricultural practices, parasitism and zoonotic risks in farmland birds (PhD project)

2025–2028 | Centre d’Études Biologiques de Chizé (CEBC–CNRS), La Rochelle University, France

This doctoral project investigates how organic farming, conventional agricultural practices and pesticide exposure shape host–parasite interactions and zoonotic pathogen patterns in farmland specialist birds. Conducted in the Zone Atelier Plaine & Val de Sèvre, the work combines field ecology, parasitology, ecotoxicology and statistical modelling to document how agricultural context relates to the diversity and prevalence of intestinal parasites, blood parasites, ectoparasites and potentially pathogenic fungi associated with birds. It also examines how these infection patterns may be linked to variation in bird condition and other host traits, while assessing the role of agricultural practices and landscape context in structuring parasite communities and organisms of potential zoonotic concern. More broadly, the project seeks to clarify how farming systems influence wildlife health in agricultural landscapes and how ecological data can contribute to a better understanding of biodiversity conservation under environmental change.

PhD supervisors: Prof. Jérôme Moreau and Dr Karine Monceau (CEBC–CNRS / La Rochelle University).

Urban greenspaces, avian diversity and zoonotic risk

University of Yaoundé I, Yaoundé, Cameroon; research across urban green spaces

The project aims to document variation in bird species richness, community composition and ecological characteristics among green spaces exposed to different levels of urban disturbance. It also examines avian parasites and selected agents of potential zoonotic importance to explore patterns of infection across bird species and habitats. Field sampling combines bird surveys and capture-based sampling, with parasitological analyses and ecological comparisons planned or under way.

The research seeks to understand the biodiversity value of urban green spaces and the ecological factors associated with parasite infections, while distinguishing the detection of potentially zoonotic agents from demonstrated human transmission risk.

Collaborators and poster co-authors: Danielle Manewoung, Anaelle Bakami, Aures Yemdji, Litila Franc, Carmel Diffouo, Yollande Gueohada, Gabriella Ondoua and Sévilor Kekeunou.

Promoting conservation of agricultural birds (ProCAB project)

Littoral Region, Cameroon · community-based farmland conservation research

This project examines how agricultural practices influence bird assemblages in farmland landscapes around Mount Manengouba in the Littoral Region of Cameroon. It combines bird surveys with information on crop management, pesticide use, shade cover and local perceptions, with the broader goal of identifying practices compatible with both agricultural production and bird conservation.

The project also has a strong applied dimension, aiming to generate locally relevant recommendations and strengthen dialogue with farmers and students on bird-friendly agricultural practices. It serves both as a research framework and as a conservation initiative linking ecological data, community engagement and training opportunities.

Collaborators: Daniella Manewoung, Crinan Jarrett, Anaelle Bakami, Prof. Sévilor Kekeunou and Dr Gonwouo Nono Legrand.

Past projects

Host stress and transmission-stage investment in avian malaria (Master 2 project)

January–July 2024 | UMR MiVEGEC, Montpellier, France

During my second-year Master’s research project at UMR MiVEGEC, I investigated how host stress may influence investment in transmission stages of avian malaria parasites. Using Plasmodium relictum and domestic canaries as a study model, the work examined variation in parasitaemia, gametocytaemia, conversion to transmission stages and sex allocation in response to experimentally elevated corticosterone.

The project explored how changes in the host physiological environment may affect parasite transmission strategies, with implications for disease ecology and host–parasite interactions. It combined experimental infection biology, parasitological measurements and ecological interpretation of parasite plasticity.

Supervisors: Ana Rivero, Claire Loiseau, Arnaud Berthomieu and Valentin Chauvin (UMR MiVEGEC). Collaboration: Frédéric Angelier (CEBC–CNRS).

Nest-dwelling ectoparasites and long-term plumage coloration in blue tits (Master 1 project)

March–September 2023 | UMR CEFE, Montpellier, France

During my first-year Master’s research project at the Centre d’Écologie Fonctionnelle et Évolutive (CEFE), I examined the consequences of nest-dwelling, blood-feeding Protocalliphora larvae for blue tits (Cyanistes caeruleus) in Corsica. These parasites develop in nests and feed on nestlings, potentially affecting early growth and the acquisition of traits later expressed in adult plumage.

The main objective was to investigate whether exposure during development leaves measurable carry-over effects on condition and coloration after fledging. The study also examined whether parasites encountered while breeding could influence adult parental coloration. We considered plumage traits with potential relevance for visual signalling, including yellow coloration and ultraviolet-blue crown coloration, and asked whether effects differed among offspring, mothers and fathers.

The research linked field-based nest monitoring, ectoparasite load assessments and quantitative colour measurements. Results highlighted moderate but persistent associations between parasite exposure and certain coloration traits, with effects depending on age, sex or breeding context rather than being uniform across individuals.

Supervisors: Claire Doutrelant, Claire Loiseau, Amélie Fargevieille and Lisa Sandmeyer (UMR CEFE, Montpellier).

Bird and bat diversity in Lake Ossa Wildlife Reserve, Cameroon (Master project)

August 2019–January 2020 | University of Yaoundé I, Cameroon

My Zoology Master’s research focused on bird and bat diversity in the Lake Ossa Wildlife Reserve, a wetland and forest-associated landscape in the Littoral Region of Cameroon. Birds and bats provide complementary perspectives on vertebrate biodiversity because they occupy partly overlapping habitats while differing in activity periods, mobility, foraging strategies and ecological roles.

The project aimed to inventory both taxonomic groups, describe their assemblages and examine their ecological use of habitats, including possible dimensions of niche overlap. Field surveys included mist-netting and complementary observations, enabling the documentation of species occurrence, community structure and variation among sampled sites. By examining birds and bats within the same protected landscape, the study sought to contribute baseline knowledge relevant to local biodiversity assessment and conservation planning.

The research also provided practical experience in tropical field ornithology, bat surveys and community-level biodiversity analysis. Such baseline surveys are particularly valuable in Central African ecosystems where biodiversity information remains incomplete and pressures on natural habitats are changing.

Supervisors: Prof. Sévilor Kekeunou and Prof. Eric Moïse Bakwo Fils. Equipment support: IDEA WILD.
