About me

I am an ecologist and PhD researcher at La Rochelle University (CEBC–CNRS, France). My research interests lie at the interface of avian ecology, host–parasite interactions, conservation biology and environmental change. I am particularly interested in understanding how human activities reshape bird communities, the organisms associated with them and the ecological processes that connect wildlife to its environment.

My research experience spans tropical and temperate ecosystems, from the biodiversity of birds and bats in Central African landscapes to the effects of parasites on avian traits and the responses of parasites to changes in their hosts. These complementary approaches have shaped my interest in connecting field natural history, ecological theory and quantitative analyses to investigate biodiversity patterns and wildlife health.

I currently focus on the ecological consequences of agricultural intensification, pesticide exposure and habitat modification, while also contributing to ornithological research and conservation initiatives in Cameroon. Through projects in agricultural and urban landscapes, I aim to develop evidence that can inform biodiversity conservation, ecological monitoring and the study of pathogens of potential zoonotic importance.
